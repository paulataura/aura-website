Monitoring and maintenance

Know what changed.
Know what happens next.

A pump station needs both reliable information and practical upkeep. ASG brings pump station monitoring, planned maintenance and fault response into one programme, shaped around your equipment and the site it serves.

We review the station, its service history and the signals available. Where telemetry is in place, we use that information alongside inspections and maintenance records to understand what needs attention. Monitoring informs the work; it does not replace visits and servicing.

A practical service programme

Look after the whole station.

01

Pump operation

Review running and stopped states, duty arrangements and reported faults alongside the condition of the equipment.

02

Wet well and floats

Include wet well levels, primary float and backup float status in the monitoring and inspection plan where these signals are available.

03

Telemetry and flow

Review available flow readings, communication status and alerts so changes can be investigated in context.

04

Planned servicing

Agree inspection and maintenance intervals around equipment, usage, site conditions and service history.

05

Fault response

Set out who receives alerts, how issues are assessed and what response arrangements apply to your site.

06

Wider site coordination

Connect station upkeep with drainage, gullies, interceptors and collections under the same asset management plan.

Questions before you enquire

Pump station maintenance explained.

Can ASG manage an existing station?

Yes, we welcome enquiries about existing domestic and commercial stations. Share equipment and telemetry details so we can review the support needed.

Can you work with our current telemetry?

We will review the provider, access arrangements and available signals before agreeing a monitoring service. Compatibility and responsibilities depend on the system.

How often should the station be serviced?

The schedule depends on equipment requirements, usage, condition and past issues. We agree the programme after reviewing the station rather than applying one interval to every site.

What if a pump is stopped?

A stopped pump may be ready for its next duty cycle or may need investigation. Fault signals, levels and the operating arrangement help distinguish normal operation from an issue.

How do I contact ASG in an emergency?

Call 01386 571556 for our 24/7 emergency contact. Tell us the site location and what the station is reporting so we can assess the issue and discuss response arrangements.
